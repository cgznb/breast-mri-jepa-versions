\title{Forecast-aware latent modeling for longitudinal prediction of pathological complete response from breast MRI}
\author{Author information to be completed}
\address{Affiliations and corresponding author to be completed}
\begin{abstract}
Longitudinal breast MRI can reveal treatment response, but endpoint classifiers do not explicitly model the future trajectory implied by an observed history. We investigate forecast-aware latent modeling for pathological complete response (pCR) prediction using three-phase dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI, baseline clinical variables, and assigned-arm scenarios. An autonomous predictor estimates future latent states, and an attention readout combines the predicted terminal state, its change, and direct history features. The cohort comprises 976 patients and 3,641 examinations, with 801 development patients and a fixed internal holdout of 175 patients, including 56 positive outcomes. Three principal variants distinguish prediction without future-state supervision, auxiliary-supervised direct prediction, and forecast-attention readout. We evaluate training seeds 44, 2026, and 3407, averaging five fold probabilities within each seed before computing metrics and then equally averaging seed-specific metrics. Forecast attention achieved mean T0/T1/T2 AUROCs of 0.7448/0.7456/0.7470 and Macro AUROC/AP/F1 of 0.7458/0.6188/0.5663, compared with 0.7293/0.5914/0.5546 for the control without future-state supervision. Its Macro AUROC sample seed standard deviation was 0.0042. A supplementary mean-pooling control had Macro AUROC 0.7446 and F1 0.5667. The forecast-attention versus control AUROC contrast had approximate paired-bootstrap $p=0.02107$ and nine-test Holm $p=0.18963$; none of the nine principal tests survived adjustment. Forecast attention had lower specificity than auxiliary-supervised direct readout (difference $-0.0430$, all-90 Holm $p=0.02880$) and worse probability-quality point estimates. These exploratory findings require independent validation because seed 42 was excluded retrospectively and the reporting procedures were chosen after results were available.
\end{abstract}
